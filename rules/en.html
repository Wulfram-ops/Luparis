Cheat sheet

Attacks and DCs

Attack: d20 + mod + Mastery (Tier + 1) against Evasion. Natural 20: double the Mastery dice.

DC: 8 + mod + Mastery. 10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard, 25 very hard, 30 nearly impossible.

Save: the threat sets the domain, the story sets the intent (Resilience by default).

Damage and boxes

Below Minor: 0 boxes, but 1 Stamina (wear). Minor to Major: 1. Major: 2. Severe: 3.

At 0 boxes: unconscious, death saves. Resistance and vulnerability apply before thresholds.

Defense

Evasion = 10 + Physical Finesse (armor cap) + shield.

Shield: +1 to +3 Evasion depending on its size, no roll. Large shields cap Finesse like armor and allow Intercept.

Effects and maneuvers

Total value ≤ Tier. Paid in Mana (spell) or Stamina (weapon).

1: heavy blow, offset, movement, light control, heal (per box). 2: heavy control, persistence, extra target (each). 3: major control, area or whirlwind (successful save = half), extra action.

Rest

Short (1 h): half the missing boxes and Mana, all Stamina.

Long (8 h): everything, and one less level of exhaustion.

Distance and terrain

1 tile = 5 ft (1.5 m), diagonals included; +5 ft per height level.

+1 half cover, +2 cover, +3 impassable; −1 difficult, −2 impassable.

Luparis plays with the D&D 5.5 rules. This book only describes what changes. Anything not written here stays the D&D rule.

1. The Matrix of Intents

The six D&D ability scores are replaced by nine cells: an intent (Power, Finesse, Resilience) applied to a domain (Physical, Mental, Social). Always name the intent with its domain: Physical Finesse, Mental Resilience. Each cell has a modifier, like a D&D ability. The context of the action picks the cell, never the school of magic.

Six stats, nine cells. You buy six stats: the three intents and the three domains. Each cell is the sum of its intent and its domain: Physical Power = Power + Physical. A stat starts at 0 and can drop to −1. Being bad at something is a choice: lowering a stat to −1 gives back 20 XP.

Cap by level: stats up to +3 until level 5, +5 until level 10, +7 until level 15, no cap beyond. XP stays the real limit: each +1 costs double the previous one.

Example: an Aegis mage who projects an imposing aura during a negotiation uses Social Power. If they forge a hammer of light to strike, they use Mental Power.

2. The character

There are no classes. Each character spends XP on the schools of magic, weapon masteries and stats they want. Their stats tell their life story, whatever their people: no race gives a bonus.

Everyone starts with a d6 hit die, then buys d8, d10 and d12 with XP.

Speed: 6 tiles (30 ft) per turn. The Fleet-footed talent adds 2 tiles per rank (2 ranks, 40 XP each). Heavy armor costs 2 tiles.

XP costs (v0.4)

Level equals XP earned divided by 100: 150 XP to create a level 1 character, 2,000 at level 20. The Character tab does all the math.

Armor and shield training: like proficiencies in D&D. Wearing armor you are not trained for gives no armor value (your thresholds are those of an unarmored body) and −2 Evasion. A shield without training gives no bonus. Medium armor needs light training first, heavy armor needs medium.

Specialization: your disciplines are ranked by Tier. The highest costs normal price, the 2nd ×1.5, the 3rd ×2, every further one ×3. At level 20 (2,000 XP), that buys one discipline at Tier 5 and a second at Tier 4, with the matrix and a d8; a tank who takes the d12 settles for Tier 5 and Tier 3.

Tier gates: Tier 2 from level 3, Tier 3 from 5, Tier 4 from 11, Tier 5 from 17, like D&D's major tiers.

3. Defense: Evasion and armor

Armor Class is gone. The attacker rolls d20 + bonus against the target's Evasion. On a hit, armor decides how much the blow costs.

Evasion = 10 + Physical Finesse (capped by armor) + shield + other bonuses.

Armor value = the armor's base AC in D&D − 10. It sets the Minor threshold and adds to the Major and Severe thresholds.

Shields

A shield adds its bonus to Evasion, with no roll. The bigger it is, the more it protects and the more it gets in the way: large shields cap the Physical Finesse that counts toward Evasion, like armor (keep the lower of the two caps). Someone in heavy armor loses nothing: it is the armored tank's natural pick; the nimble duelist keeps a small shield.

Intercept (reaction): when an adjacent ally is attacked, the attack targets your Evasion instead of theirs; if it hits, you take the blow. Once per round; each extra Intercept costs 1 Stamina.

Wear: a blow stopped by armor costs no box, but it costs 1 Stamina. When Stamina is empty, each stopped blow costs a box. Nobody is invincible against numbers.

5. Mastery and magic

Spells and weapons follow the same rule. Your Mastery in a school or a weapon is your Tier, from 1 to 5. It sets how many dice you roll; what you use sets the die type.

  1. Basic attack, free. Weapon: Mastery × weapon die + modifier. School's basic spell: Mastery × the school's die, no modifier (see the table of schools).
  2. Effects. Each effect is worth 1 to 5. The total costs that much Mana and cannot exceed your Tier in the school.
  3. Circumstances. A bonus or a penalty changes Mastery by ±1: an inspiring ally, Rage, a weakened or blinded target.

Apex

The Apex is abnormal physical power: it is not magic, but it is not normal either. It is bought by Tier like a weapon family (same XP costs and Tier gates).

  • Breath: each Apex Tier adds Breath exactly like a weapon family (1, 3, 7, 12, 18 cumulative).
  • Natural armor = 2 × Apex Tier (2 to 10). It does not stack with worn armor: keep the better value. Natural armor never limits Evasion.
  • Infusion: the Apex can infuse a weapon strike (see below).

Infusion (v0.3)

A character who masters both a weapon family and a source of power can infuse the strike: the strike deals extra damage. The infusion has a value from 1 up to your Tier in the source, and each point costs 1 Mana (a school) or 1 Breath (the Apex) and adds one die: the school's damage die (d8) for a school, the weapon's die for the Apex. Paid when the attack is declared, even on a miss; the extra dice are added to that strike's damage. The source is a magic school (a Crucible adept sharpens or densifies a cutting blade, an Aegis adept wraps a mace in solidified energy) or the Apex (a blow struck with outrageous strength). One infusion per strike.

Damage types come from the story. At character creation, the player and the GM agree on what the character's school can produce: Aegis, being solidified energy, will hardly deal anything but force, bludgeoning or physical damage; a Surge adept may deal force, fire or radiant damage, depending on their lore and what they know how to do. The scene can tighten or widen those limits.

Expertise

Pick a precise field: lockpicking, tracking, herbalism, the history of Luparis, sailing… Your checks in that field add +2, +4 then +6 (ranks 1 to 3, for 20, 40 then 80 XP). Buy it for as many fields as you like. It is the answer to "how good is my character at this", outside combat.

8. Rolls, DCs and conditions

Rolls follow D&D, with Mastery replacing the proficiency bonus.

  • Mastery bonus = Tier + 1 (+2 to +6).
  • Attack roll = d20 + cell modifier + Mastery bonus, against Evasion. On a natural 20, double the Mastery dice.
  • Spell or maneuver DC = 8 + modifier of the cell used + Mastery bonus.
  • DC of an effect left on the ground (flames, acid, ice, webs…) = 10 + its author's Resilience, in the domain the story points to (physical, mental or social). On the map, set the DC next to the Effect tool.
  • Elevation on the battle map: a tile at elevation 1 is difficult terrain; elevation 2 needs a jump or a climb; elevation 3 needs a high jump (only characters strong enough), flight or a climbing check. A jump uses Physical Power, a climb Physical Power or Finesse, DC set by the GM.
  • Resistance and vulnerability halve or double damage before comparing it to thresholds.

10. Game Master's chapter

Converting a D&D monster

Evasion = the monster's AC. Thresholds, boxes and Mastery follow its CR smoothly, with no jumps between tiers. The Bestiary on this site does the math automatically.

  • Major = 7 + CR (rounded); Severe = 2 × Major.
  • Boxes = HP ÷ (4 + CR), rounded, at least 1.
  • Mastery Tier of its attacks = 1 + CR ÷ 4.5, rounded, between 1 and 5 (CR 1: 1, CR 5: 2, CR 10: 3, CR 15: 4, CR 17+: 5).

Example: an ogre (CR 2, 59 HP): Major 9, Severe 18, 10 boxes, Tier 1. A young red dragon (CR 10, 178 HP): Major 17, Severe 34, 13 boxes, Tier 3.

The rest of the stat block

  • Abilities: Physical Power = STR, Physical Finesse = DEX, Physical Resilience = CON; Mental Power = INT, Mental Finesse = average of INT and WIS, Mental Resilience = WIS; Social Power and Finesse = CHA, Social Resilience = average of WIS and CHA (modifiers).
  • Proficient saves: STR, DEX, CON give the Physical domain; INT, WIS the Mental; CHA the Social. Add the Mastery (Tier + 1).
  • Attacks and DCs: the D&D proficiency bonus is replaced by the Mastery (Tier + 1). Weapon damage: Tier dice of the weapon die + modifier.
  • Breaths and recharge abilities: the GM chooses the recharge, 1 to 3 turns. Damage = Tier dice per turn of recharge: every turn, 1 × Tier dice; with 3 turns of recharge (as in D&D), 3 × Tier dice. A Tier 5 dragon breathes 5d6 every turn, or 15d6 every 3 turns. The recharge drops by one turn at the start of each of its turns.
  • Other damage (auras, poisons, extra damage on a hit) keeps its D&D dice. Compared to thresholds, it costs at most 3 boxes.
  • Distances in tiles (5 ft = 1 tile); healing and hit points in boxes (HP ÷ (4 + CR)).
  • A condition "for 1 minute, save again each turn" becomes a persistent condition.
  • In Luparis, every dragon is chaotic neutral.

Ruling an unexpected effect

Find the D&D spell closest to the player's idea. Its level gives the value: levels 1 to 3, same value; 4 and 5, value 4; 6 and up, value 5.

Terrain

On the tactical map: +1 half cover, +2 cover, +3 impassable obstacle; −1 difficult terrain, −2 impassable.
